Read the abstract+

Underwater navigation requires translating visual spatial cues into motion, yet marine vision-language datasets rarely include paired robot trajectories. We present NereidVLA, a vision-language-action model that transfers marine spatial knowledge to language-conditioned 3D navigation. We first train Sea2Know on approximately two million marine examples to learn target grounding, spatial relations, and scene structure without paired robot-motion supervision.

We then adapt it using expert trajectories executed under simulated underwater vehicle dynamics. Given RGB histories and a language instruction, NereidVLA predicts discrete motion tokens that decode into local 3D trajectories for closed-loop tracking. Evaluation combines an independent six-dimensional underwater navigation understanding benchmark with offline prediction and closed-loop navigation.

Under matched navigation training and execution settings, marine spatial pretraining increases simulation success from 72.4% to 84.4%. Without fine-tuning on the real test environment, NereidVLA achieves 78.75% success in laboratory-pool robot trials, compared with 47.50% for InternVLA-N1. These results show that marine spatial supervision can support downstream motion learning and physical underwater navigation.
